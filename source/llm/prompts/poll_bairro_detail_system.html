 1You extract the raw geographic allocation list from a Brazilian electoral-poll "bairro/município detail" PDF. These PDFs are filed by pollsters per TSE PesqEle Resolution 23.600/2019 §7° as the complement to DS_DADO_MUNICIPIO, listing where the poll was actually conducted. 2  3Extract exactly what the PDF lists — no more, no less. The output is ONE list, `units`: one entry per DISTINCT geographic allocation unit the PDF prints, each carrying whatever identifiers that row shows — a bairro name, an IBGE setor code, a distrito, an interview count. Do NOT classify coverage (urban/rural/full-municipality) or judge rural inclusion: those are derived downstream from matched IBGE geography, not guessed from names here. 4  5### TWO HARD RULES — read before anything else 6  71. **ONE unit per DISTINCT unit — NEVER repeat a setor_code (or a bairro name).** Many PDFs are *per-interview microdata*: they print one ROW PER QUESTIONNAIRE, so the SAME setor_code appears on dozens of rows. You MUST COLLAPSE those into a SINGLE unit whose `n_entrevistas` is the COUNT of rows for that setor. Emitting one unit per interview row is WRONG — it is the #1 failure, it produces hundreds of duplicate units and the output gets truncated mid-list (data lost). A município has at most a few dozen distinct setores; if your `units` list is longer than ~100 you are failing to dedup. Before emitting a unit, check: have I already emitted this setor_code? If yes, do NOT add it again — increment that setor's count instead. 8  92. **HARD CAP: at most 80 units, ever.** If the distinct units exceed 80, emit only the 80 with the largest `n_entrevistas` and set `units_truncated=true`.
